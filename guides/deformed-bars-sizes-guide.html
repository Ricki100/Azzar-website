Deformed Bars in Zimbabwe: Sizes, Uses and How to Order

Ordering reinforcing steel is easier when you know what the sizes mean. Here is a guide to deformed bars, mesh and how to order the right amount.

Azzar Fencing and Steel · Updated 27 October 2026 · 4 min read

Quick answer

Deformed bars (rebar) are steel reinforcing bars with raised ribs that grip concrete. They are named by diameter: Y10 is a 10 mm high-yield deformed bar, Y12 is 12 mm, and so on. Smaller sizes such as Y8 to Y12 are common in slabs, lintels and light structures; larger sizes such as Y16 to Y25 are used in columns, beams and heavier foundations. The size and spacing must come from your engineer, builder or approved plans. Order using a bill of quantities if you have one.

What are deformed bars?

Concrete is strong when squeezed but weak when pulled or bent. Steel bars cast inside the concrete carry those pulling forces. Deformed bars have raised ribs along their length so the concrete grips them and the two materials work together. Plain round bars (often marked R, for example R8) have a smooth surface and are mostly used for links and stirrups.

Reading bar sizes

In Zimbabwe, bars are usually described by a letter and a number:

  • Y means a high-yield deformed bar, and the number is the diameter in millimetres. Y12 is a 12 mm deformed bar.
  • R means a plain round mild steel bar. R8 is an 8 mm round bar.

The table shows the standard mass per metre for each size, which is how steel is often weighed and priced. It is calculated from the diameter, so it is the same for any supplier.

Y1010 mm0.617 kgSlabs, lintels, ring beams, boundary walls
Y1212 mm0.888 kgSlabs, beams, footings, columns in light structures
Y1616 mm1.578 kgColumns, beams, heavier footings
Y2020 mm2.466 kgHeavily loaded columns and beams
Y2525 mm3.853 kgLarge structural members

Quick weight check: mass per metre (kg) = diameter in mm × diameter ÷ 162. So a Y16 bar weighs about 16 × 16 ÷ 162 = 1.58 kg per metre.

Deformed bars or reinforcing mesh?

Deformed barsReinforcing mesh
What it isIndividual bars, cut and tied on siteA ready-welded grid in sheets
Best forColumns, beams, footings, complex shapesFloor slabs, driveways, yards, some walls
SpeedSlower: every bar is placed and tiedFaster to lay over large flat areas
FlexibilityAny layout the engineer specifiesFixed grid sizes

Many projects use both: mesh in the slab and deformed bars in the foundations, columns and beams.

Where steel goes in a typical build

  • Foundations and footings: bars in strip and pad footings carry the building's load into the ground.
  • Floor slabs: mesh or bars help control cracking and carry loads.
  • Columns and beams: main bars run along the length, tied with links.
  • Lintels and ring beams: carry loads over openings and tie walls together.
  • Boundary walls and gate pillars: reinforcement stops cracking and toppling, especially on tall walls or walls carrying a gate or fence.

How to order the right amount

  1. Use your bill of quantities or drawings. They list each bar size, number and length.
  2. Allow for laps and waste. Bars overlap where they join, and offcuts are unavoidable.
  3. Order by project stage (foundations first, then slab, then columns and beams) if storage on site is limited.
  4. Store steel off the ground and covered, so it stays clean and free of heavy rust.
  5. Plan delivery access for a truck carrying long bars.

Always follow your engineer's or builder's specification for bar sizes, spacing and cover. Using smaller or fewer bars to save money can make a structure unsafe.
